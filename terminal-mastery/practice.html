Vim practice

Click into a buffer and press actual Vim keys. Par is computed by searching every possible route, so it is the true minimum, not my guess.

How to use it

Click the code, then type. Cyan is your cursor, pink is the target. Get there in as few keys as you can. Esc clears a half-typed count. The gutter shows relative numbers, exactly like your real config.

Do this once a day for a week and motions stop being something you think about.

1 · Left, right, up, down

Only h j k l and counts. Read the gutter - it is telling you the number to type.

2 · Words

w next word, b back, e end of word. Capitals ignore punctuation, so W treats foo.bar() as one blob.

3 · Within a line

0 column zero, ^ first real character, $ end of line. Then fx jumps onto the next x, tx stops just before it. F and T go backwards.

4 · Across the file

gg top, G bottom, { } jump by blank line, % hops to the matching bracket. 12G goes to line 12.

5 · Everything at once

Real code, no restrictions. Beating par here means you are actually fluent.

6 · Compose an edit

Motions get you there; operators change things. Type the keys you would press. This is the grammar - operator + text object - and it is the thing that makes Vim worth learning.

What this trainer does not cover

Registers, macros, marks, and :global. All powerful, all wrong to learn before motions are automatic. Ask me when you are ready.

It also simplifies two things: w and b cross line boundaries here exactly as in Vim, but $ lands on the last character rather than past it, and there is no operator-pending mode. Neither difference will mislead your fingers.
